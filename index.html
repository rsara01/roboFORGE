Build a robot arm and teach it to pick things up.

This one's running the same inverse kinematics code as the full simulator. Move your mouse over it and it reaches for your cursor. On a phone, tap where you want it to go.

How does it know where to reach?

It doesn't, at first. It guesses. It takes the joint closest to the tip and turns it until the tip points at the target. Then it moves one joint back and does the same thing, all the way to the shoulder, and starts over. After a few passes the tip lands within a few millimetres.

That's called cyclic coordinate descent, or CCD, and in RoboForge it's under 100 lines of JavaScript. Drag the orange dot. The faint lines are the arm's earlier guesses, so you can watch it get closer.

Or just tell it what to do.

The Arm Lab has a Script tab that runs plain JavaScript, and these are real commands from it. ikTo(x, y, z) moves the middle of the gripper to a point (in meters). Closing the jaws with gripper(0) picks up whatever is between them, and opening them lets go.

You can edit it first.
