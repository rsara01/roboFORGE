§ 02 · the solver

It's real math, not an animation.

Nothing here is a recording. To reach a point, the arm runs a solver called CCD: starting at the wrist and working back to the shoulder, it turns each joint so the tip points a little closer to the target, then repeats until it's close enough.

Drag the orange dot. Slow it down to watch it think, one joint at a time.

Every arm in RoboForge reaches this way, including yours.

§ 03 · scripting

Talk to it in code.

The Arm Lab has a script tab that runs plain JavaScript. This box uses the exact same commands: moveTo turns one joint, ikTo reaches for a point in space, gripper and grab do what you'd hope.

edit it. break it. press run.

§ 04 · import

Bring your own robot.

Robots are usually described in a format called URDF: a list of links, joints and 3D meshes. Hand RoboForge one and it becomes a working arm you can drive, solve IK on, script, and use to pick things up.

Drop any robot arm's URDF here.

A .urdf with its meshes folder, a whole folder, or a .zip of the robot package.

.urdf · .zip · STL · DAE · OBJ · GLB

no URDF handy? try one of these →

Sample arms are open-source robot descriptions; each one's license and credit is listed with it.
